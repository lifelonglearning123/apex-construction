§ How we work in Melksham

Modern stock, modernising at pace.

Melksham's housing tells the story of postwar British development: 1950s council estates, 1970s mass-build semis, 1990s/2000s executive estates around Bowerhill, and a wave of post-2010 development from Berryfield north to Beanacre. It's a town defined by movement.

Most of our SN12 work is on this newer stock — extensions, garage conversions, kitchen renovations and patio/driveway upgrades on properties where the original spec is starting to feel dated. The structural fabric is generally good (modern foundations, cavity walls, properly damp-proofed), so projects move faster than the period work in Bath or Bradford-on-Avon.

The A350 corridor — Beanacre, Lacock, the new Hampton Park developments to the south — has driven steady demand for garage conversions and home offices since the shift to remote work. We've fitted out dozens of these.

§ SN12 specifics

What we know about building here.

A

Wiltshire Council planning

Same council as Trowbridge, same predictable approach. Most extensions on Melksham estate stock fall under PD. Conservation areas (central Melksham, Lacock) need full planning.

B

Modern housing stock

Most properties post-1960. Cavity-wall construction, modern foundations, proper damp-proofing. Means extensions tie in cleanly and structural surprises are rare.

C

The garage-office boom

The single most common project we run in Melksham. New estates have integral garages that are perfect for office conversion — already insulated walls, modern foundations, easy ethernet routing.

D

A350 access

Most SN12 properties are within 10 minutes of the A350. Materials deliveries and skip swaps are easy. Site logistics simpler than the period-property areas.
