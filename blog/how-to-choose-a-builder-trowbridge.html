How to Choose a Builder in Trowbridge — 9 Questions to Ask Before You Sign.

The nine questions that separate a builder you'll regret from one you'll recommend. Use it on us. Then use it on whoever else you're quoting.

Most extension regret stories start the same way: "the quote was the cheapest." It's almost never the actual reason it went wrong, but it's almost always the doorway through which the real problem walks. Cheap-quote builders cut corners somewhere — usually somewhere structural, which you can't see until you can.

The nine questions below are the ones we'd ask if we were hiring a builder ourselves. They're designed to tease out the things that go wrong. Apply them honestly to every builder you quote — including us.

1. "Are you a registered limited company, and what's your Companies House number?"

Real construction businesses are registered companies. Sole traders can be excellent, but they offer less recourse if things go wrong. A limited-company builder gives you a public record at find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk — accounts, directors, registered address, incorporation date.

2. "Where is your office?"

Builders without a proper office aren't necessarily bad, but they're harder to hold accountable. A real registered address is one of the cleanest signals that someone is invested in their reputation locally. Vans and home addresses can disappear overnight.

3. "Where can I read independent reviews?"

Not testimonials hand-picked for the builder's own website (those can be cherry-picked). Look for reviews on independent platforms — Google, MyBuilder, Houzz, TrustATrader. Most require the reviewer to confirm a completed job before posting.

Pay attention to volume as well as rating. 5/5 from 8 reviews is less reliable than 4.7/5 from 200. Look for patterns in what reviewers consistently praise (or criticise).

Ours are collected after handover and published unedited — read them on our reviews page.

4. "Can I see two of your finished projects in person?"

Photos are easy to fake. Walking around a finished extension someone else has lived in for two years is not. A builder confident in their finish should happily put you in touch with past clients within a few miles.

If a builder offers only video walk-throughs or a "we'll send pictures", probe further. Most happy customers are willing to spend 20 minutes showing you a job done well — it's how Wiltshire word-of-mouth has always worked.

5. "What insurance do you carry, and can I see the certificates?"

Two non-negotiables:

  • Public Liability Insurance — protects you if their work damages your property or injures someone. Industry standard is £2m–£10m cover.
  • Employer's Liability Insurance — legally required if they have employees on your site.

Ask for current certificates by email before signing anything. A real builder will send them within a day. A reluctance is a flag.

6. "Will you handle planning, building regs and structural calcs as part of the quote — or are those extras?"

The most common quote-comparison trap. A "£45k extension" from one builder may include nothing but the build; the same scope quoted at £58k from another may include drawings, planning submission, structural calcs, BC fees and decoration. The headline figures are wildly misleading until you compare like-for-like.

Ask every builder for an all-in figure: drawings, planning, building control, calcs, materials, labour, decoration and VAT. Then compare those.

7. "Show me a written, line-by-line quote — not an estimate."

The single most important difference. An estimate is a "best guess". A quote is a fixed-price contract figure that doesn't move unless you change the spec. A line-by-line breakdown lets you see what's included and removes the "I didn't realise that wasn't included" surprises that lead to mid-build rows.

If a builder won't put a fixed total on paper, walk away. Verbal estimates and "we'll see how it goes" pricing are how budgets break.

8. "How are payments structured? When do you ask for the deposit?"

Healthy structure for a £50k+ project:

  • Small deposit on contract signing (5–10%) — covers first material orders.
  • Staged payments tied to completed milestones: foundations passed BC, shell complete, first fix complete, second fix complete, final completion.
  • You withhold the final 5–10% until snags are signed off.

Red flags:

  • Deposit over 25% before work starts.
  • "Half upfront, half on completion" — too much sunk cost.
  • Cash-only requests with no VAT receipt.
  • Pressure to pay outside normal channels.

9. "What's your guarantee, and what happens if something fails after handover?"

Industry-standard workmanship guarantee is 12 months. Manufactured items (boilers, kitchens, appliances) carry their own warranties — typically 2–10 years. Structural elements get implicit cover via Building Regs sign-off (and the BC certificate is what mortgage lenders ask for years later).

For really expensive projects (£100k+), some clients pay extra for a third-party Insurance-Backed Guarantee that survives the builder going out of business. Ask if it's available.

The best builders welcome questions. They've answered them all before. The ones who get defensive are usually the ones who've got something to defend.
