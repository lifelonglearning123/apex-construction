Do I Need Planning Permission for an Extension? Wiltshire & Somerset Rules 2026.

Permitted development limits, conservation-area gotchas, and the real differences between Wiltshire Council, B&NES and Somerset Council on the ground.

The first question every extension client asks is the same: "Do I need planning?" The honest answer is "it depends" — but it depends on a small number of clear factors. This post walks through every one of them for the three councils that cover our service area: Wiltshire Council, Bath & North East Somerset (B&NES), and Somerset Council.

The two routes: PD vs full planning

Every extension goes through one of two regulatory routes:

  • Permitted Development (PD) — extensions within size limits set nationally don't need a full planning application. You can either build straight away, or apply for a "Certificate of Lawfulness" (~£129 + drawings) which gives you written proof in advance. We always recommend the certificate — it costs almost nothing and saves an enormous headache when you sell.
  • Full Planning Permission — required for anything outside PD limits, anything in conservation areas with PD rights removed, all listed buildings, and anything in flats/maisonettes (PD doesn't apply at all). Application fee in 2026 is £258 for a householder application. Plus Listed Building Consent if applicable.

The PD limits — the headline rules

Single-storey rear extensions

  • Detached house: up to 4m deep from rear wall, max 4m at ridge.
  • Semi-detached or terraced: up to 3m deep, max 4m at ridge.
  • Width: no wider than the original house if the extension reaches a side boundary.
  • "Larger Home Extension" route (neighbour consultation scheme): up to 8m deep for detached, 6m for semi/terrace — but neighbours can object and the council reviews.

Two-storey rear extensions

  • Up to 3m deep from rear wall.
  • Must be at least 7m from any rear boundary.
  • Roof pitch must match the original house.
  • Materials must be similar to existing.

Side extensions

  • Width: no more than half the width of the original house.
  • Single storey only under PD — two-storey side always needs full planning.

Volume / coverage limits

  • Total ground coverage of all extensions can't exceed 50% of the curtilage (your land minus the original house footprint).
  • Eaves can't exceed 3m if within 2m of any boundary.
Important: PD rights can be removed

If your property is in a conservation area, is listed, or has had its PD rights removed via an Article 4 Direction (common in some Bath conservation areas), you cannot rely on PD — even if your project is within size limits. Always check before drawings.

How the three councils differ in practice

Wiltshire Council (covers Trowbridge, Bradford-on-Avon, Melksham, Devizes, Chippenham, Westbury, Warminster)

Pragmatic and predictable. Most PD certificates issue cleanly within 8 weeks if drawings are accurate. Full planning typically 8–10 weeks. They use a clear pre-application advice service (£180 in 2026 for householder enquiries) which we recommend if you're near any limits.

Conservation areas in BA14 (parts of Trowbridge town centre and St James's), BA15 (most of central Bradford-on-Avon, the Tory, Trinity Road, Newtown), SN12 (central Melksham, Lacock village), and SN10 (parts of Devizes) all have PD rights restricted. Listed buildings need full LBC.

Bath & North East Somerset (B&NES) — covers Bath BA1, BA2

The strictest in our service area, by a margin. Bath is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and almost every property within the BA1 ring is in a conservation area. Many have Article 4 Directions removing PD rights for materials, windows, doors and roofs.

Conservation Officers care intensely about: scale, rooflines visible from public viewpoints, window proportions, materials (Bath stone matching where applicable), and historic curtilage. Allow 12–14 weeks for full planning, longer for LBC.

Pre-application advice is essentially mandatory for anything other than the smallest job — fees range from £180 to £600+ depending on scope. We strongly recommend it.

Somerset Council (covers Frome, Shepton Mallet, Wells, Mendip)

Successor to the former Mendip District Council since 2023. Service is improving but processing times are still 10–14 weeks for full planning — slightly slower than Wiltshire at peak. Conservation officers are responsive but firm.

Frome's town-centre conservation area (Catherine Hill, the Trinity area, around St John's Church) has Article 4 restrictions on materials and window changes. Many central properties are listed. The same goes for Wells, parts of Shepton Mallet, and most rural village cores.

Six common gotchas that catch people out

  1. "My neighbour built one bigger" doesn't mean you can. They may have had planning approval. Or they may be in breach. Or PD limits may have changed since.
  2. Article 4 Directions don't show up on a basic property search. Always check directly with the council planning portal before assuming PD applies.
  3. Building Regs is separate from planning. Even a PD project needs BC sign-off. Don't conflate the two.
  4. Listed status can apply to outbuildings. A listed barn within your curtilage can put restrictions on the main house too.
  5. Trees protected by TPOs. You can't lop or fell a TPO'd tree without consent — and that includes within 5m of a major foundation.
  6. The "8m larger extension" rule isn't automatic. Neighbours can object during a 21-day consultation. If they do and the council agrees, you have to redesign or apply for full planning.

How Apex handles planning

For most clients, the planning route is part of our quote. We:

  • Confirm whether the property is in a conservation area, has Article 4 Directions, or is listed — using the council planning portals — before drawings start.
  • Produce drawings to the standard the relevant council requires.
  • Submit and manage the application on your behalf.
  • Respond to officer queries during the consultation period.
  • Adjust drawings if conditions are imposed.

For complex listed-building work in central Bath or Bradford-on-Avon, we partner with a heritage architect from the start — the additional cost is well worth it for the smoother LBC process.

If your project is borderline on PD limits, pay the £129 for a certificate of lawfulness. Future buyers' solicitors ask for it. Without it, you may have a sale stuck in limbo years later.

The practical timeline

For a project that needs full planning:

  • Weeks 1–3: Survey, drawings, planning consultation with the council if needed.
  • Weeks 4–6: Submit application. Council validates within 5 working days.
  • Weeks 6–14: Public consultation period (3 weeks), case-officer review, possible site visit, decision.
  • If approved: Implement within 3 years.
  • If refused: Right of appeal within 12 weeks; alternatively redesign and resubmit.

For PD with a certificate of lawfulness: 6–8 weeks total from submission to certificate.
